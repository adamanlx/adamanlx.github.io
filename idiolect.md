## Cot-caught near-merger

My COT and CAUGHT vowels are really contrastive before coda [l] - *stock* [stɑk], *stalk* [stɔ(l)k]. But I also think I have a true underlying [l] in the *-alk* words: in careful speech, *talker* can syllabify as [tɔl.kɹ̩].

Outside of that, who even knows. I have been told by true unmerged people that I produce a difference, but I struggle to hear it myself. I think I'm usually more confident about [ɑ] words, but most words sound okay with both [ɑ] and [ɔ]. For example, *bot* has to be [bɑt], but *bought* could be [bɑt] or [bɔt].

## Before [l]

Before [l], /i/ becomes [ɪ], or it might be [ʲɪ]. *Feel* and *fill* are both, for all practical purposes, [fɪl]. But if I'm thinking about it, *feel* might be [fʲɪl] in careful speech.

Where others have [ʊl] I more often say syllabic [l̩].

I monophthongize everything before [l]. /aɪl/ is [ɑl], /ɛɪl/ is [ɛl], /aʊl/ is [al], and /oɪl oʊl/ are both [ol]. Maybe /oɪl/ is juuuust a hair fronter or less rounded than /oʊl/. Because I do a lot of cooking, people often notice that I say "cooking oll." I get a lot of smack for saying cooking oll.

When people pronounce vowels I *don't* have before [l], I often hear a syllable break. *Feel* as [fil] to me sounds like [fi.jl̩] "fee-yul." There's something about the formant transition to that heavily velarized [l].

Given how reduced my vocalic inventory is before [l], it's perhaps surprising that I *don't* participate in some mergers: [ul], [ʌl], and [l̩] all contrast such that *pool*, *gull*, and *pull* don't rhyme. I think *gull* is the only word I have with [ʌl]. Even *seagull* has [l̩].

## Lexical / syntactic variation

For me, *with yall* is most felicitously contracted into [wɪtʃɑl]. I also really hate spelling it *y'all* with the apostrophe. *Y'all* feels so corporate for some reason.

If I've already used *yall* in a sentence I tend to re-refer to the group with *you.*

I use *ain't* and negative concord. While I generally use third-person singular *-s* I'll happily use *don't* instead of *doesn't.* I say *don't/ain't got* instead of *don't/doesn't have* a lot, but I don't think I say *didn't/ain't got* for *didn't have.*

I often prefer demonstrative *them* instead of *those.*
